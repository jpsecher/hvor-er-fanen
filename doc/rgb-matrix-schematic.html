Before you route

The lid is what makes charging work, so the interlock is a functional part and not a convenience. Termination is 7.5% of the programmed current, which is 22.5 mA, and a blank display already draws 244 to 294 mA from the same node. With the display powered the cycle never completes, and at sprite brightness the cell discharges while nominally charging — silently, because no firmware can read STAT. Blanking the panels in software does not help either, since the 224 mA floor is 256 always-running controllers. SW1 has to be open, and coupling it to the lid is what guarantees that.

  • F1 must be time-lag, and it must stay slower than the electronic trip. It exists for one fault the AP9101C cannot see — a MOSFET failing drain-to-source, which leaves the protection silently gone. Everything else is already caught faster and resettably, so the fuse must never act first. Without soft-start the bound came from inrush: 1000 µF through about 50 mΩ gives an 84 A peak, so each switch-on was I² t ≈ 0.18 A²s and repeated pulses accumulated, wanting a melting I² t of at least 0.9 A²s. Q3 removes that, cutting each switch-on to a few thousandths of an A²s, so the fuse is now sized only against the fault it exists for. Keep it time-lag regardless: a 2 A fast-blow sits close to normal operating transients and will fatigue open. Put it on clips rather than soldered, and keep the cell-side track no longer than the holder tab.
  • Star ground at P−. The panel return and the NodeMCU return each reach P− on their own path. Panel current swings by more than an ampere as pixels change, and if the ESP sits downstream of that, its ground reference moves with the picture — and the DIN threshold is measured against exactly that reference.
  • Pour thick copper on both sides of the cell path: cell positive through SW1 to the panels, and panel ground through P− and the two MOSFETs back to the cell negative. The regulator branch is a few hundred milliamps and needs nothing special.
  • Both MOSFET tabs are the same node. TO-263 tabs are the drain and the two drains are common here, so they share one pour with no isolation between them. Dissipation is 36 mW across the pair, so the pour is for soldering rather than cooling.
  • The mode input has no pull-up resistor; the pin provides its own. pinMode(D1, INPUT_PULLUP) is what holds it high, and with C9 that gives a debounce time constant of 3 to 10 ms — the internal pull-up is roughly 45 kΩ but loosely specified. Tactile bounce is commonly 1 to 5 ms, so there is margin, and the slower release edge is imperceptible. The internal pull-up exists only once pinMode has run, so the pin floats through boot, which is harmless on a pin that is not a strapping pin.
  • The mode button is not tied to D1, but it cannot go just anywhere either. Because the design leans on the internal pull-up, the pin must have one — which rules out D0 (GPIO16), the only ESP8266 pin without. It must also not be a strapping pin: a button to ground on D3 (GPIO0) or D4 (GPIO2) stops a normal boot if it is held at power-on, and D8 (GPIO15) has to be low at boot and carries a board pull-down that an internal pull-up would fight. With D2 (GPIO4) taken by the panel data, that leaves D1, D5, D6 and D7, and D6 is pencilled in for charger STAT. So D1, D5 or D7, freely.
  • The two CC pulldowns are what make the port work at all. A USB-C source supplies nothing until it sees a pulldown on a CC pin, so R9 and R10 must both be fitted, one per pin rather than one shared between them, because that is how the source detects the cable orientation. Leave them out and a C-to-C cable delivers no power while a legacy A-to-C cable still works, so the port supplies nothing with some cables and works with others. They ask for nothing beyond the default 5 V, which covers the 300 mA the charger draws.
  • Put both USB connectors behind the same lid. Flashing through the NodeMCU socket with the cell connected puts its onboard AMS1117 on the 3.3 V rail alongside U3, and USB and the cell must never drive that rail together. That is the same fault class as charging with the display on, so one lid covering both ports closes both with one mechanism.
  • Q3 soft-starts the panel supply, and takes the current off SW1. The 1000 µF would otherwise draw an 84 A peak through the switch contacts on every close, which erodes and welds them — a wear mechanism the protection analysis does not cover, since the surge decays long before the AP9101C could act. C10 ramps the gate over about 9 ms, so the capacitor charges at C·dV/dt ≈ 0.42 A instead, and SW1 is left carrying only the 38 µA of gate current. That means the mechanism can be a microswitch or a reed switch and magnet rather than a 3 A toggle. It still fails safe: a jam with the lid shut prevents charging, a jam with it open prevents switch-on. Turn-off runs through R11 alone and takes 100 ms, which is why R11 is not made larger to improve the gate drive.
  • Q3 is an AON7407, chosen on gate drive rather than on current. R11 and R12 divide the cell, so VGS is about −3.8 V when full and −2.9 V at 3.2 V, and it can never exceed the cell voltage. The AON7407 is specified below 12.5 mΩ at −2.5 V and characterised down to −1.8 V, with a −0.9 V worst-case threshold, so it is fully on across the whole range and costs 19 mV at the 1.5 A worst frame. If you substitute, the clause to search on is RDS(on) specified at VGS = −2.5 V — the common SOIC-8 parts are standard-level and fail here, an AOD403 or DMP3013SFV would switch off as the cell drained, and a BSS315P is far too small. Size the package for the fault as well: up to 8 A for 10 ms passes through Q3 before the protection acts.
  • Measure the AP9101C overcharge threshold before committing the layout. VCU detects between 4.200 and 4.250 V while the MCP73831-2 regulates between 4.168 and 4.232 V, and those ranges overlap. Ramp a bench supply across VDD and VSS from 4.0 V and watch CO. If the parts in hand detect at 4.240 V or above there is enough margin.
  • Keep the LDR where it cannot see the panel. A photocell facing a 16×16 display is a feedback loop — bright frame, high reading, brightness stays up. Put it on a face pointing away from the front, or recess it in a short tube. This decides whether the feature works, more than the choice of part does.
  • R8 shares its node with the NodeMCU's own divider. The board carries roughly 220 kΩ and 100 kΩ from the A0 header, so about 320 kΩ to ground sits in parallel with R8 — 100 kΩ becomes about 76 kΩ in practice. Measure the board's actual resistors before sizing R8, since NodeMCU variants differ. The LDR is on the top leg for this reason: on the bottom leg that 320 kΩ would swamp the dark end, which is the range night dimming depends on.

What this sheet does not decide

Pin numbers are shown only for U2 and U3, where they were confirmed against the datasheet and the top marking. For U1 and the MOSFETs, take the numbering from the KiCad symbol and check it against the datasheet when you pick the footprint.

SW2 is momentary, so it says nothing about the mode at power-on: the display starts in sprites and each press cycles it. Because SW2 carries no power, D1 is polled in the loop rather than read once at boot, so a mode change costs no reboot and no WiFi reassociation. The charger STAT pin is drawn unconnected, since no firmware runs while charging; the 470 Ω and LED indicator from VBUS to STAT can be added if you want a visible charge light.
